# calculus-refresher

### A concise set of problems to refresh Calculus

The refresher problems in these notes were written independently by me,
often inspired by mathematical topics and techniques encountered while
studying Marsden and Weinstein's *Calculus* series.

They are not reproductions of the textbook exercises. Numerical values,
wording, examples, and problem formulations have generally been changed
or created independently.

## Personal Views

A traditional U.S. public school path through mathematics, and where
calculus fits into it, can be outlined roughly as follows.

1. **Elementary school: Kindergarten through Grade 5**

   In India, what is called a "class" in the United States is usually
   called a "grade."

   During these years, children are introduced to basic arithmetic,
   fractions, geometry, measurement, and other elementary mathematical
   ideas.

   My personal preference is for a more rigorous and problem oriented
   style of early mathematics education, particularly the style I have
   seen in older Soviet mathematics books. I find that approach more
   appealing because it encourages children to think deeply about simple
   ideas rather than merely practice procedures.

2. **Middle school: Grades 6 through 8**

   Mathematics gradually becomes more substantial here, with greater
   emphasis on fractions, ratios, equations, geometry, and eventually
   algebra.

   My impression is that students of a similar age in countries such as
   Romania, China, Russia, and India are often exposed earlier to more
   demanding problems and a wider range of mathematical topics.

3. **High school: Grades 9 through 12**

   In high school, students may progress through algebra, geometry,
   trigonometry, precalculus, and eventually calculus.

   For context 9th grade is called Freshman, 10th Sophomore, 11th Junior,
   and 12th is called Senior. It is confusing for folks who studied in India.

   Advanced Placement courses include **AP Calculus AB** and
   **AP Calculus BC**. These are optional courses rather than part of the
   standard curriculum for every student.

   AP Calculus AB is primarily an introductory course in single variable
   differential and integral calculus. AP Calculus BC goes further and
   includes additional topics such as sequences and series, parametric
   equations, and polar coordinates.

   When I compare this with my own education in India, much of what is
   covered in AP Calculus BC feels similar to material I first encountered
   around the 11th and 12th grades.

## Looking Back

One thing I deeply appreciate about studying mathematics in the United
States is the extraordinary quality and availability of books and other
learning resources.

The textbooks available to students here are, in many cases, far better
than the books I had access to when I was a student. There are also
libraries, used books, online lectures, freely available course material,
mathematics competitions, and an enormous amount of specialized material.

An interested student can keep learning almost without limit.

When I was growing up in India, the environment felt very different.
There was much more emphasis on following a prescribed academic track,
doing well in examinations, and competing for a limited number of
opportunities. The resources available to me, especially books, were far
more limited.

Sometimes I wish I had had access to better books, less pressure from
competition, and greater maturity in how I approached studying.

But that is only a retrospective thought.

I am fortunate for the education I did receive, grateful for what it
allowed me to accomplish, and especially grateful that I now have the
opportunity to return to mathematics simply because I enjoy it.

These notes are partly a record of that return.

They are not meant to be a calculus textbook or a complete set of
solutions. Instead, I am collecting a relatively small number of
carefully chosen problems, derivations, formulas, and ideas that can act
as triggers for memory.

My hope is that years from now I will be able to open these notes, solve
a handful of representative problems, and quickly reconstruct the major
ideas of calculus in my mind.

## The Marsden and Weinstein Calculus Books

The authors, Caltech, and Springer have made the three volumes of
Marsden and Weinstein's *Calculus*, along with related student guides
and errata, available online.

They can be found here:

https://www.cds.caltech.edu/~marsden/volume/Calculus/

The original books remain copyrighted by their respective copyright
holders. This repository contains my own study notes and independently
written refresher material and does not redistribute the textbook PDFs.
